## Motivation

What problem this solves, and why the current state is unsatisfying. Concrete beats abstract:
the thing that does not work today, and what it costs.

## Study

How the problem is solved elsewhere, and what those solutions cost. This is the section that
justifies the proposal's existence — a proposal is first and foremost a place to **study**.

Prefer measured facts over recalled ones. A claim that a mechanism behaves a certain way is a
hypothesis until something exercises it; say which it is.

## Proposed design

The design that fits, with its trade-offs stated rather than hidden. Alternatives considered
belong here, each with the reason it lost.

## Decision

The outcome and its reasoning. A rejected alternative is recorded with the argument that
retired it — including the ones that were *deliberately dropped*, so their absence is not read
as an oversight later.

Where a resolution rests on a measurement, name it and link the evidence.

## Rollout

What must happen for this to exist, in order, including what is verified and how.
